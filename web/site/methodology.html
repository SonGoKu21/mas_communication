METHODS SUPPLEMENT

Extended methodology

From a communication intervention to an auditable outcome.

This supplement expands the paper’s taxonomy and two-phase fault-injection pipeline. It connects the methodological definitions to implementation snapshots and recorded examples. The framework is broader than the conditions evaluated in any one experiment.

Method definitions follow the author-supplied Methodology text, 25 September 2026. Results remain aligned with manuscript revision 7. No new experiments are reported here.

01 · SCOPE OF THE MODEL

A cross-layer taxonomy

P / D / N / T

Where a lower-stack disturbance originates.

→
A · Receiver exposure

What changes at the receiving application boundary.

→
M · MAS consequence

How affected information changes downstream state or behavior.

The mapping is conditional: lower-stack disturbances may be masked, and different sources can produce the same visible symptom. A-layer faults can also originate directly in agent-runtime delivery, ordering, association or structured-message handling. Local model reasoning, parameters, activations and training-state errors are outside this taxonomy.

Coverage boundary. The table is a classification, not a claim that every entry was injected in every benchmark. The 3,780 source-mapping executions are controlled fixtures; they do not carry MAS task-outcome scores.

02 · PHASE I — FAULT GENERATION

Intercept a concrete communication dependency

Select the task, workflow, model configuration, repetition, operator and parameters. Identify the selected edge, carrier and task/session association. The interceptor acts after sender-side production and before receiver-side consumption:

φf,θ(mt, St) → (m′t, S′t)

Here m is the carrier and S is the relevant communication/runtime state. Clean runs use the same instrumentation with an identity operation. Provider requests/responses, local model internals and tool semantics are outside the intervention boundary.

I2 · Action request

An upstream instruction or tool request enables subsequent work. Losing it can prevent evidence from being generated.

Inspect: requested action, sending edge, delivery count, downstream work.

I3 · Observation

An environment or tool result returns to the agent workflow. Delayed, displaced or incomplete observations can change available state.

Inspect: tool result, state version, ordering and actual receiver input.

I4 · Evidence handoff

Produced results reach a verifier or decision agent. A fault here changes access to, or interpretation of, existing evidence.

Inspect: evidence carrier, receiver decision, alternate information paths.

Step 2/3/4 are abstract dependency types, not necessarily the second/third/fourth event in a trace. Eligibility varies by operator. The common matrix tests I2 and I4; describing I3 does not imply it is included in that matrix.

03 · WHAT WAS CHANGED

Operator implementation and evaluated conditions

The common matrix contains clean plus six condition-position cells. Two cells are non-delivery at different positions; this is not a count of six distinct fault families. Expand a cell for its intervention and exposure check.

timeliness_deadline_step4Timeliness · I4

A handoff exceeds its configured delivery budget and is not delivered.

Exposure check. Inspect deadline_ms and the delivery-event record. Do not infer exposure from elapsed run time alone.

non_delivery_step2Availability · I2

The selected action request is withheld before receiver consumption.

Exposure check. An unreached target is not applied. Trace the work that the missing request would have enabled.

non_delivery_step4Availability · I4

Generated evidence is withheld on the selected downstream handoff.

Exposure check. Inspect other recorded edges: evidence may remain available through a bypass path.

semantic_corruption_step4Semantic integrity · I4

Task-critical meaning in a processable evidence carrier is changed.

Exposure check. The replacement depends on the domain adapter. Compare original/delivered fields and subsequent use; a parseable message is not necessarily correct.

valid_partial_message_step4Completeness · I4

A processable carrier retains an answer or partial content while required evidence is removed.

Exposure check. Inspect the task-specific contract. Missing evidence does not by itself mean the numeric answer is wrong.

stale_replay_step4Freshness / association · I4

Prior-task or prior-session evidence is supplied at the current handoff.

Exposure check. Inspect task/session/version metadata and actual payload differences. A replay request without prior evidence does not establish changed exposure.

Broader implemented operator inventory

The generic registry also lists delay, timeout, channel interruption, endpoint unavailability, omission, message corruption, malformed JSON, truncation, duplication, reordering, schema mismatch, stale replay, runtime communication-state corruption, message-channel instruction injection and contract violation. Generic scenario handling additionally includes valid-partial and partition-equivalent cases. This inventory is not the main-study evaluation matrix.

Examples from the generic interceptor: truncation keeps the first half of serialized content; malformed JSON creates an unfinished wrapper; schema mismatch renames selected keys; contract violation changes selected field types; valid-partial removes product/evidence keys. These are implementation examples, not universal semantics for every domain adapter.

Generic FaultScenario defaults are delay_ms=1000 and timeout_ms=500. They are constructor defaults, not verified parameter values for every published run. Use each run’s fault_parameters and event records for actual settings.

Legacy A1–A15 codes and names belong to particular implementation snapshots. Read the recorded descriptive symptom and local registry together; do not treat those numeric codes as a universal mapping to the paper’s seven A-layer families.

04 · PHASE II — PROPAGATION EVALUATION

Keep exposure, propagation and outcome separate

VariableRequired evidenceWhat does not establish it
A · ExposureOriginal/delivered carrier differences or a delivery-event trace confirming the intended receiver-visible change.A scheduled fault, a configuration flag alone, or final task failure.
M · ConsequenceA trace-linked downstream state or behavior change. Semantic consequences require affected information to be accepted or used.Changed text that no downstream agent uses; task failure alone does not establish the propagation path.
O · OutcomeThe task evaluator’s result under that adapter’s evaluation contract.Inferring failure from A or M. A successful endpoint can coexist with a consequence.
B · Handling responseA recorded handling reaction after exposure.A complete detection–correction–state-restoration chain or a verified recovery rate.
Decision procedure and boundary cases
  1. Check whether execution reached the intended event. If not, record not applied.
  2. Verify actual exposure, including delivery count/timing for missing carriers. Preserve an unknown state when the available archive cannot establish the change.
  3. Follow the dependency through actions, aggregation, accepted evidence, verification and termination. For omission, identify the required action or state that can no longer complete.
  4. Read the independent evaluator result. Keep correct-answer and evidence-contract requirements distinct when the adapter combines them.
  5. Report the historical handling flag separately. Do not relabel it as strict recovery.

These are the paper’s interpretation criteria. Historical labels are retained as recorded; this website does not claim fresh independent annotation or complete re-adjudication. Some legacy labeling rules incorporate decision/completeness/outcome fields. Inspect their code and the trace before interpreting a label as independently measured causal evidence.

Changed but rejected

A may be established while a particular semantic-acceptance M label is unsupported. Rejection can still alter workflow behavior.

Propagated yet successful

A recorded consequence and final success can coexist; another path may supply useful evidence.

Correct answer, failed contract

An endpoint may fail because required evidence is missing even when a candidate answer is correct.

06 · EXPERIMENT PROTOCOL

Reproduce the artifact and understand its limits

Common-matrix design

100 tasks × 3 model configurations × 3 topologies × 7 conditions × 3 repetitions = 18,900 executions. The seven conditions are clean plus the six cells above. Tasks span Shopping (30), Admin (20), Reddit (15), SWE-bench (20) and TheAgentCompany (15). Repetitions identify repeated executions; stored traces do not establish independent sampling seeds.

Matching and denominators

For a clean/fault contrast, match collection, domain, task, model, topology and repetition. Report the differing condition and any other changed dimensions. Applied-fault analyses exclude scheduled-but-unreached interventions; overall endpoint summaries and clean-conditional losses have different denominators. A clean-conditional loss conditions on successful matched clean executions. Preserve the analysis script’s aggregation and missing-pair rules when reproducing a reported percentage.

Keep the Reddit extension (2,835), source-mapping fixtures (3,780), and RQ4 factorial (864), diagnostic (90) and finite-exposure (144) studies separate. Two planned exposures do not guarantee two realized exposures. SWE/TAC fault trials use frozen evidence rather than a fresh end-to-end benchmark execution per fault.

The current export indexes 26,613 records and bundles detailed evidence for 6,800. Summary-only records do not supply a full trajectory. Field presence varies by collection and historical runner. Separate logs retain recorded order and are not artificially synchronized; absent fields are not reconstructed. Deployment identifiers are redacted.
